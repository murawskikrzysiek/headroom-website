Why ACX rejects audiobooks

ACX checks three things in every chapter and rejects on any of them.

Loudness and peaks show on most meters. The room-tone noise floor does not: it is inaudible on headphones, invisible on a loudness meter, and a common reason an audiobook comes back. Specula reads all three from the file, per chapter, and judges them as ACX states them, so a reading exactly on a limit fails.

ACX RMSA volume between −23 and −18 dB RMS, per chapter. ACX checks plain RMS, not K-weighted LUFS, so Specula judges RMS here too.
ACX peaksPeaks less than −3 dB, read on the 4× oversampled true peak, so −3.0 dBTP fails. With the TP threshold at −3 dBTP, every breach, a mouth click or a page-turn thump, shows on the waveform where it happened.
ACX floorA noise floor less than −60 dB RMS, measured over the blocks that are not speech.

Deliver to Audiobook makes ACX the primary target and keeps it checked, with Apple Podcasts and Spotify (Podcast) beside it. The card words a failure in ACX's terms: "Fails ACX (Audiobook) on the noise floor: −58.2 dB RMS. ACX requires a noise floor less than −60 dB RMS." A long narration under another category gets a note under Deliver to, such as "This is long-form speech in 12 chapters.", with a switch to Audiobook.

Score the book

Clean the speech map, split the chapters, score them, export.

1 · Clean the speech map
Silero's neural speech detector marks the speech when the file opens. Where a breath, a page turn or a music bed is mislabelled, the Speech task (⌘5) corrects the regions (drag, I and O, split with S), so the noise floor and the speech readings measure the right blocks. The corrections are saved beside the file and come back when it reopens.
2 · Split into chapters
The Chapters task (⌘4), opened on a book without chapters, detects and measures them, or offers the chapters saved in an earlier session. Detect (⌘D) splits the file at silences, 2 s under −55 dB by default (Detection, in the task bar), and asks before replacing chapters that were named or moved. Drag a boundary on the chapter map to correct it, remove one (⌫) to merge two chapters, rename them, or Import a layout from a JSON file.
3 · Score every chapter
Analyse (⌘L) measures each chapter's RMS, integrated loudness, true peak, noise floor, LRA, max short-term, dialogue loudness and Speech %. The table groups them as ACX (RMS, true peak and noise floor, each green or red), Consistency (each chapter against the book's median, in RMS or LUFS as chosen in Detection, ±2 by default) and Details. The ACX summary in the sidebar counts them: "3 of 20 chapters fail ACX." After an edit the chapters are measured again, and until then the table judges nothing.
4 · Hand off the receipt
File › Export… (⌘E) writes the PDF, HTML and JSON reports and the chapters CSV into one folder. The report's chapter table carries the same groups, each ACX cell green or red, with ACX's limit as its tooltip in the HTML report, and the report names ACX as what the book was judged against.

Per chapter, and against the median

A file-level pass is not enough for a book. An audiobook can sit at −20 dB RMS overall while one chapter recorded on another day drifts to −16, or one quiet pickup dips under the noise-floor line. ACX checks per chapter, so every chapter gets its own verdict on RMS, true peak and noise floor.

The consistency flag catches what the absolute limits miss. Each chapter is compared with the book's own median, in RMS by default, and one beyond the tolerance (±2 by default) is flagged even inside ACX's window: the chapter that would sound louder than the rest to a listener mid-book.

The corrected speech regions from step 1 feed this: each chapter's dialogue loudness and noise floor measure the speech and the pauses as corrected.

Fix what's flagged, in the same app

Each red cell points at its fix, and the two flags that bounce the most audiobooks each have one.

A noise floor at or over −60 dB RMS is a common ACX rejection, and a uniform gain cannot fix it: raising the voice raises the room tone with it. Level dialogue, in the Speech task's sidebar, works from the other side. Using the corrected speech regions, it ducks the tone between phrases first and then lifts the voice to a target, so the floor lands lower while the narration comes up, and before Apply it predicts the dialogue loudness and the floor, with the floor against ACX. It covers a floor that just misses, a dB or two over the line; hiss in the capture needs a new take.

A chapter that drifts off the book's loudness is the consistency flag, and Level chapters, in the Chapters task's sidebar, is the one-move fix: each chapter's gain to the book's median or to a fixed target, in the metric the chapters are compared by (RMS, ACX's, or LUFS), shown chapter by chapter before Apply, with a true-peak ceiling that starts at −3.1 dBTP for ACX. Chapters › Level Chapters (⇧⌘L) applies it as one edit. A true peak at or over −3 dBTP takes the card's Limit peaks to −3.1 dBTP, which trims the overshoots and leaves the chapter levels. Every fix is an edit in the Edit task's History. The editing workflow →

Before the take

The room check. Armed, the Record task (⌘7) reads the room's floor over the last five seconds, measured the way the file's noise floor will be, and judges it against ACX's limit, so a humming room shows before a chapter is recorded. Punch-ins at the playhead keep the file's length, and speech is detected again inside the punch. Recording →

In the app

Batch a whole series

A folder of chapter files in one run.

File › Check a Folder… under Deliver to Audiobook checks every file in a folder against ACX: a row per file with its verdict, integrated loudness, true peak, LRA and Speech %, measured with the app's speech settings and any corrected regions saved for the file, a report per file and a CSV of the rows, written into a new folder beside the checked one.

The command line and Shortcuts measure the same numbers and take a loudness mode (music, podcast, vod or broadcast), judging its default targets; ACX's verdict comes from the app and from Check a Folder. Batch and automation →

Before you upload

The chapter table says which chapters clear ACX and which do not, and the two flags that bounce the most books each have a one-move fix: Level dialogue for a marginal room-tone floor, Level chapters for the chapter that drifts off the book's loudness.
