Task Spectral ⌘6

When there's no take two

Everything about the take is right, except one moment.

A cough lands under a line of dialogue, a chair creaks between two piano chords, a burst of mains hum rides under a quiet passage, and everything else about the take is right. Re-recording means getting the room, the performer and the take back, and sometimes that is not possible: the client has left, the location shoot wrapped, the interview subject is not coming back, the source is an archival transfer.

The fix that problem needs is narrow: find the blemish in time and frequency, select exactly that, and process only the selection, leaving everything around it untouched. That is a different job from a general noise-reduction pass or an EQ move across the whole file, and it is usually priced and packaged as its own thing.

What that job costs elsewhere

How it works

Select the blemish, pick an operation, audition before Apply. All of it on a zoomable, full-resolution spectrogram.

1 · Select the blemish
⌘6, or Spectral in the top bar, turns the canvas into a full-resolution, zoomable spectrogram of the open file, sharing the other views' zoom and scroll. Four tools sit in a palette on its left edge and share one selection: Rectangle (R) for a time and frequency box, whose outline shows the cells it will take while it is dragged; Lasso (L) for a free shape; Brush (B) for painting over a curved trace such as a bird chirp or a whistle; and Wand (W), which outlines, dashed, what a click would take: the connected region within its tolerance above or below the level under the pointer, reaching 250 ms each side by default. A drag with the rectangle or lasso, or a click with the wand, replaces the selection; Shift adds and Option subtracts. Brush strokes add, and an Option-stroke removes. The selection has a white outline, the rest of the canvas dims, the feather's roll-off shows as a soft edge around it, and its time span and frequency range print beside it; Esc clears it. The Floor and Ceiling knobs on the task bar (or Spectral › Contrast…) set the levels drawn black and brightest, to bring a faint blemish into view. For stereo files the canvas shows the left channel, the right or both stacked; multichannel files edit one channel at a time, picked by name from the file's layout, and the selection stays across channel switches.
2 · Choose the operation
The operation panel in the sidebar holds the operation and its amount, the feather, the channels Apply edits, Solo, Hear Result and Apply. Attenuate lowers (or raises) the selection by a set amount, default −18 dB. Erase (Spectral › Erase to Floor, ⌫) pushes it down to the noise floor learned from the audio just before and after it, less an erase depth (default 12 dB), so removal stays decisive inside busy material. Repair bridges it in time, interpolating each frequency's level from the clean audio on both sides, for a short blemish crossing steady material. Every selected cell gets the full edit, the feather's roll-off lies outside the selection, where the canvas draws it, and phase is never touched. Apply edits all channels by default, or the ones picked. The analysis size (1024 to 8192) trades time detail against frequency detail; changing it with a selection asks first.
3 · Audition, then apply
Space plays the selection's span. Solo (⇧Space) plays only the selection, with the feather the operation would use, so what an Erase or an Attenuate would touch is heard first. Hear Result (⌥Space) plays the chosen operation as Apply would write it. One thing plays at a time: Space stops an audition, and starting one pauses the file. Spectral edits join the Edit task's history, and the selection survives an Apply and an Undo, for another pass. Save As writes a new file; the task bar's Save writes over the original once that is allowed. Selections longer than 10 minutes are refused; select a shorter range and apply in passes. FAQ

Is the Spectral task an automatic cleanup tool?
No. The Spectral task is manual spectral editing: the problem shows on the spectrogram, a rectangle, lasso, brush or wand selects it, and one of three operations, Attenuate, Erase or Repair, applies to the selection. There is no automatic de-noise, de-hum or de-reverb pass at this time. The fix lands exactly where it is pointed, and nothing else changes.
Do I need a subscription to use spectral repair?
No. Specula is a one-time purchase, and the Spectral task is part of the $99.99 price, not a separate add-on or a higher tier. There is no recurring fee to keep using it.
How does it compare to the dedicated spectral repair tools?
Same core job, select a region on a spectrogram and fix it, different scope and price. The dedicated tools bundle spectral repair with a wider set of restoration modules at a specialist price. Specula bundles the Spectral task with the rest of its analyzer, the delivery checks, loudness, FFT, spectrogram, compare, edit and record, at $99.99, one time. Neither is a strict subset of the other; the choice follows which set of tools the job needs.

Fix the moment, not the take

The Spectral task is for the blemish inside an otherwise good recording: select it on the spectrogram, in time and frequency, and process just that region. Its edits join the Edit task's history, and Save As writes a new file.
