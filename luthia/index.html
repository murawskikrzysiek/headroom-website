Luthia

A practice suite for guitarists, on Mac, iPad and iPhone. It opens the exercises a teacher hands out (Guitar Pro, MusicXML, MuseScore, MIDI, a pasted tab), plays them against a click, and prints under every note how many milliseconds early or late you landed. Chords and the fretboard are drilled by playing them: the app listens and moves on when it hears the answer. A weekly plan holds it together and adjusts to how the week went. The library follows you through iCloud.

Why Luthia

A metronome says where the beat is. Luthia says where you were, note by note, in milliseconds.

Most practice apps ship their own curriculum. Luthia is built to sit next to a teacher's: the course files land in a folder, every exercise opens in the same editor, and the click and the timing work on all of it. Guitar Pro 3 through 8, MusicXML, MuseScore, MIDI and pasted ASCII tab all import, with the written fingering kept and every simplification listed before the file is saved.

The timing is measured, not judged. The click, the input and the score share one clock; each pick is found on the attack, matched to the note it was meant for, and reported as a signed number of milliseconds. A session reports two things: the spread of those numbers, which is what practice changes, and the lead, how far ahead of the click you sit, which is the human default; it is reported and left alone. There is no score and no star rating.

Chords and the neck are drilled with the instrument in hand. The chord drill names a chord and waits until it hears it; the fretboard drills ask for a note, an interval, a degree, a chord tone, a triad or a note on the staff, and accept the answer played or tapped. Every drill also runs with no guitar at all, from name chips, so the theory half works on the train.

The app is free. Every mode and every mechanic run on the free tier; a single in-app purchase, Luthia Pro, opens the content library and the planner, on every device at once.

Five modes

One window, ⌘1 through ⌘5. The metronome and the tuner sit beside whichever mode is open.

Compose (⌘1)
A tab and staff editor, keyboard-first: digits for frets, a column of digits for a chord, +/− for duration, dots, triplets, rests, techniques on single keys. The library of built-ins and your own exercises in folders. Generate, Import and Export live here.
Train (⌘2)
Play an exercise in a loop against the click. Per-note deviation in milliseconds, session spread and lead, a row per loop, pitch verdicts, a playhead that sweeps the score. Detailed below.
Chords (⌘3)
Every quality in every key as diagrams with strum playback and function labels; a live meter that names what you are playing; a flashcard drill that listens; progressions, built-in and your own, in any key.
Fretboard (⌘4)
The whole neck with overlays for notes, a scale, a chord's tones, intervals, octaves and the scale boxes, re-rooted by a tap. Six drills answered by playing, tapping or naming, with a per-position heat map.
Practice (⌘5)
A weekly plan of timed blocks across eight areas, a timer that opens each block where it runs, a log with ratings, and suggestions that adjust tempo, position and pool from the ratings.

Train

Per-note deviation in milliseconds, spread and lead for the session, a row per loop, all on one clock.

Listen, click, Space. The count-in is yours: the session anchors to the first onset it hears and the loop starts on the nearest bar line. From then on every pick is matched to the note it was meant for and the deviation lands under that note on the score, last and running average, negative for early. A first note played a little early rolls into the next loop instead of being dropped, and an exercise with repeats is played as written, every pass of a repeated note folding into the same statistics.

Spread and lead
Spread is the standard deviation of the signed deviations; lead is their mean. Each completed loop is a row: the spread as a bar on a fixed 40 ms scale (green to 12 ms, amber to 25, red beyond), the lead as a dot above the line for late and below for early, and coverage, matched notes over expected, as the bar's opacity.
One clock
The click records where each scheduled beat lands on the output, plus output latency. The input tap stamps each buffer on the same clock, minus input latency. Nothing is anchored to a guess about when the first note was heard.
Finding the pick
A pick is found on a sharpened envelope where a ringing low E is near silent and an attack is a spike, traced back to where the attack began, and confirmed over a short window so a fretting finger landing before the pick, or the click bleeding into a microphone, is not counted as a note.
Pitch verdicts
Pitch is read 30, 60 and 100 ms after the attack; the note is what it settled to. Verdicts compare pitch classes, so a low string reading its second harmonic through a microphone is not marked wrong.
Score follow
A green playhead sweeps the system. The page scrolls in lines, or the score lays out as one continuous system that scrolls sideways under a fixed playhead. Click a note and Preview starts from there, with the techniques leading into it still sounding right.
Speed and gap trainers
The speed trainer steps the tempo every N bars, or every N loops of a session, exactly on the bar line, with the timing grid retimed at the boundary. The gap trainer mutes the click for N bars out of M while the beat dots keep moving.
History
Every session that completed a loop is kept and folded per day: sessions, time, loops, pooled spread and lead, pitch accuracy. It syncs with the library.
Pinned
Through the string synth, a take rendered a known amount late reads that late on every note within 2.5 ms between 80 and 200 BPM, with every pitch verdict right. Real microphone takes are replayed through the same chain in the test suite.

Import, compose, export

The teacher's file is the exercise. Nothing is retyped.

Guitar Pro
Guitar Pro 7 and 8 (.gp) and Guitar Pro 3, 4 and 5 (.gp3 to .gp5). Techniques, bends, ties, tempo changes, key, pickup bars, repeats and section markers are read. Verified against MuseScore's own reading of real course files.
MusicXML & MuseScore
MusicXML (.musicxml, .mxl) from any notation app, and MuseScore 3 and 4 files (.mscz, .mscx) directly. A linked tab staff is folded into the notation part so the written fingering wins and the slurs, staccatos and text survive.
MIDI & ASCII tab
MIDI quantised to a grid you pick; ASCII tab pasted as text, with the usual markings (5h7, 7p5, 5/7, 7b9, 5~, x) and rhythm read from the spacing.
Fingering
Unfingered notes get a string and fret from a search over the whole line that keeps the hand in position and favours open strings; fingering written in the source is kept as written.
What changed
Every simplification the import had to make is listed before the file is saved: an unsupported technique, a merged tie, a voice that was dropped. A preview plays and can be trained without saving.
Compose
Tab and staff in step. Chords by typing a digit on another string; hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides, bends, vibrato, palm mute, let ring, dead notes, staccato, slurs and ties on single keys; key signatures, pickup bars, repeats that play back nested, rehearsal marks. On iPhone and iPad a hold opens a two-ring touch wheel with the whole palette in one gesture.
Strings that behave
The preview is a physically modelled string, acoustic or electric with an amp stage. A hammer-on is the same string re-tuned, a pull-off a weak pluck, a slide a glide, a palm mute a short decay. Chords strum low to high and held strings ring on.
Export
MusicXML with a notation staff and a TAB staff, every technique written the way MuseScore, Guitar Pro, Dorico, Sibelius and Soundslice read it; or a Luthia file that opens verbatim on another device. Any exercise, built-ins included, and never gated.

Chords & fretboard

Drilled by ear: the app listens for the answer.

Chord library
Twelve qualities in every key, from power chords to half-diminished sevenths, as diagrams with strum playback, the formula, and chord-function labels (R, 3, ♭7) on the detail view. Curated open shapes plus root-6 and root-5 movable shapes anywhere on the neck.
Live chord meter
Names what you are playing out of all 144 chords. The spectrum's peaks fold to twelve pitch classes and every candidate is scored by how much of the sound it explains and how many of its tones are present, so G minor and G5, or C and Cmaj7, stay apart.
Chord drill
Pick a pool (open majors, open sevenths, E-shape barre chords, and more), with or without the diagram hint. A chord name comes up, you play it, the app hears it and moves on.
Progressions
I-IV-V, I-V-vi-IV, the 12-bar blues, ii-V-I, the Andalusian cadence and more, in any key with the boxes for that key and looped playback; your own progressions beside them; any progression becomes a drill of its chords.
Fretboard map
The whole neck in the tuner's tuning: 6-, 7- and 8-string guitars, 4- to 6-string basses, drop and open tunings. Overlays for every note, a scale, a chord's tones, intervals from a root, octave shapes and the generator's scale boxes. Tap a spot to hear it; in a rooted overlay the tap re-roots the neck and the legend re-reads.
Six drills
Notes (find it, or every octave of it; name the dot), intervals (same string, next string or anywhere; name the interval between two dots), degrees of a key and scale, chord tones, triads in a given inversion on three adjacent strings, and staff reading. Answer by playing (pitch and octave are checked, never the string), by tapping the neck, or from name chips with no guitar in hand. One prompt per bar of the click, if you want the pressure.
Progress
A heat map of the neck: first-try accuracy and response time per position, totals per drill. Weak positions are sampled more often, so the map steers the next prompt.

Practice

The week, and what to do in it.

The plan
Seven days of timed blocks across Warm-up, Rhythm, Scales, Arpeggios, Chords, Progressions, Fretboard and Repertoire. Each block carries what it opens: a library exercise, a scale in a key and position, a rhythm workout, a drill pool, a progression, a fretboard drill. Keys rotate by fourths; short days keep every block at ten minutes or more.
The session
Start opens the block's mode and the timer, which keeps running across modes and shows in the header. Done asks how it went: struggled, OK, solid. The log keeps every block with its rating; the header counts the streak and the week's minutes.
Rebuild week
Minutes a day, practice days, a focus (Balanced, Technique, Chords & songs, Rhythm & reading) or a weight per area, and content pickers per area: which warm-ups, which scales and keys, which drill pools and progressions, which repertoire folders. The log and the streak survive a rebuild.
Suggestions
Two struggled ratings in a row propose −8 BPM or −5 minutes; three solid propose the next position, +6 BPM or the next pool; an area that went missing gets ten minutes on the lightest day. Apply or dismiss, one by one.

Free and Pro

One in-app purchase, $19.99. Mac, iPad and iPhone on one purchase, Family Sharing on. No subscription.

Free
Every mode and every mechanic: the microphone, the notation, the millisecond timing, the metronome and tuner, every import format, export, iCloud sync. The 32 built-in exercises and one exercise of your own at a time (replace or delete it to make room; unsaved previews and Train runs are unlimited). Major and minor pentatonic scales in boxes, major arpeggios, the rhythm pyramid. The curated open chord shapes in every quality, the open-majors drill, three progressions. The Notes and Scale overlays on the map, the Notes drill to fret 5. The default practice week with its timer, log and streak.
Pro
An unlimited library in folders, so a course stays together. All twelve scales, three notes per string, group and interval patterns; every arpeggio quality; rhythm figures and random grids. Every movable chord shape, every drill pool, every progression and your own. Chord tones, intervals, octaves and boxes on the map; all six fretboard drills across the whole neck, paced by the click. Rebuild week, block editing and the plan's suggestions.
ⓘ

Nothing already in the library is ever locked. Whatever it holds stays usable, trainable and editable, on every device, whatever the purchase state; the free tier's limit applies to adding. Locked items stay visible with a lock and open the purchase sheet when tapped.

Mac, iPad, iPhone

One app
The Mac layout on the iPad, with every editor key on a hardware keyboard. A compact shell on the iPhone: the modes in the tab bar, the metronome as a one-line strip, the panels as sheets, the touch wheel for editing. The click keeps running through a screen lock.
Input
Any Core Audio input on the Mac. On iPhone and iPad the built-in microphone, a wired headset, a USB or Lightning interface, or a Bluetooth microphone, in measurement mode, which keeps the system's automatic gain, echo cancellation and noise suppression out of the path between the guitar and the detectors.
iCloud sync
Exercises, progressions, the plan and log, fretboard progress and Train history follow the Apple Account. Each record is its own file, per-device logs merge, and an edit collision goes to the newer version. Off in Settings for a single device. Nothing reaches the studio.
Files
Luthia is registered for every import format, so a course file opens from the Finder, from Files, from AirDrop or from Mail.

Requirements

System
macOS 26, iPadOS 26 or iOS 26.
Input
A guitar into an audio interface gives the cleanest timing numbers. The built-in microphone works for every mode; with the click playing out loud, headphones keep it out of the measurement.
ⓘ

All analysis is local. Audio is never recorded for anyone else, and the library lives on your devices and your own iCloud account. Privacy policy.
