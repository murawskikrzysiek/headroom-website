Specula

Audio analysis and repair for macOS. Specula judges a file against where it is going: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, ACX, Netflix, EBU R128 or a spec of your own, with the verdict in a sentence and the fix that would change it. Seven tasks cover the rest of the work: inspect the file, edit it, compare up to six versions down to the residual, check audiobook chapters, correct speech regions, repair in the spectrogram with a squeak's overtones included, and record.

Deliver to

Every file is judged against where it is going, in a sentence, with the fix that would change it.

Monitors won't flag what delivery QC will: integrated loudness half a dB over Spotify's target, a dialogue mix four LU under Netflix's speech-gated level, one channel with its polarity flipped that nobody caught on headphones. Those numbers are already in the file.

Deliver to names where the file is going, Music streaming, Podcast, Audiobook, Film and TV or Broadcast, and the targets checked there, one of them primary. The delivery card under it gives the primary target's verdict as a sentence, such as "Spotify turns this down by 5.0 dB." or "Apple Podcasts plays this as recorded, 8.7 dB under its −16 LKFS reference.", with the reading in the target's own terms: integrated loudness, dialogue loudness, or ACX's RMS and noise floor. The card judges the whole file as soon as it has loaded.

Fixes in words. The primary target's fixes are buttons that say what they do, such as Limit peaks to −1.0 dBTP, Normalize to −14 LUFS and Hear at Spotify's level, offered only where they change something. Every other checked target keeps its fixes in a Fix menu.

Two checks for streaming. Under Music streaming the card also says whether a stereo master folds to mono, such as "Mono-compatible. Correlation +0.62 over the file.", and what Apple Music's AAC encode does to it: the true peak and the samples over full scale after the encode, with Limit peaks set to leave room for it.

ACX in its own words. A volume between −23 and −18 dB RMS, peaks less than −3 dB and a noise floor less than −60 dB RMS: a reading exactly on a limit fails, as ACX states it, and Limit peaks for ACX aims at −3.1 dBTP.

Seven tasks

One task at a time, each with its own tools, its own views and its own part of the sidebar.

The task switcher sits in the top bar, ⌘1 to ⌘7 reach the tasks, and Esc, with nothing left to clear, returns to Inspect. The file, its zoom and its scroll stay as they were from task to task.

Inspect

⌘1

The file as it is. The waveform, the loudness with the primary target drawn as a dashed line, and the spectrogram computed for the whole file. The sidebar holds the file, the delivery card, Loudness details with a Live side for what is playing, and the speech, music and channel readings. ⌘Return measures a selection into a card of its own.

Edit

⌘2

Cut, trim, fades and gain on the task bar; Normalize, Limit True Peak and Level Dialogue in its Loudness menu; a chain of Audio Unit effects rendered as one edit. A selection shows its length and its common edits right over the waveform. The sidebar lists the history: every edit by name, up to 16 steps back, any of them a click away. Save As writes a new file, and saving over the original is a setting.

Compare

⌘3

Up to six versions of a mix or a master sit in a matrix of their readings, each with its difference from A. Level match, sample-accurate Align, and the residual: everything that differs between A and a slot, playable with D and measured on its own. A file at another sample rate switches in without a pause, and Apple Music's encode of the master can take a slot of its own.

Chapters

⌘4

Chapters found at the silences, then a table of every chapter's readings with ACX's marks, an ACX summary for the book, and Level chapters, which brings every chapter to a common RMS or LUFS and shows each chapter's gain before it is applied. Chapters can also come from a song's sections.

Speech

⌘5

Silero's speech regions, corrected by hand: add, split, mark in and out, delete. The dialogue loudness and the speech percentage follow the corrected regions. Level dialogue predicts the dialogue loudness and the noise floor before it is applied, and judges the floor against ACX.

Podcast →

Spectral

⌘6

The spectrogram at full resolution, with a rectangle, lasso, brush or wand to select a cough, a squeak or a hum. Include harmonics adds the selection's tone at 2×, 3× and up, where it rings, so one stroke on a squeak's lowest line takes its overtones too, following the squeak as it glides. Attenuate it, erase it to the noise floor around it, or repair it from the audio either side; Solo plays the selection and Hear Result plays the edit before Apply. Audio beyond the selection and its feather stays bit for bit.

Spectral repair →

Record

⌘7

Any input, armed with live meters and a room check against ACX's noise floor before the take. A take lands as a new file, appended, inserted at the playhead or punched in; a punch at the playhead keeps the file's length, and speech is detected again where it landed. Takes stay for seven days.

Live meter

The standard itself, running in this page.

Below is a working BS.1770-4 meter. The page synthesizes a 30-second program - verse, quiet passage, loud chorus, verse, fade - and meters it as it plays: K-weighted momentary and short-term, gated integrated loudness, true peak at 4× oversampling. Specula runs the same standard natively, on your files.

The pipeline

A folder at a time

Check a Folder (File › Check a Folder…) checks every audio file in a folder against Deliver to: one row per file with its verdict, integrated loudness, true peak, loudness range and Speech %, measured with the app's speech settings and any saved speech regions made for the file. Reports in JSON, HTML or PDF are written for each file as it finishes, and a CSV of the rows at the end, into a new folder beside the checked one and never over a file.

The same engine runs from the terminal: five subcommands, analyze, compare, edit, generate and report, return the numbers the app shows as deterministic sorted-key JSON. Install it from Specula › Install Command-Line Tool…

specula
$ specula analyze master.wav
$ specula compare revA.wav revB.wav --match-loudness --out-diff residual.wav
$ specula report mix.wav --mode music --format pdf --out report.pdf

Shortcuts and the Finder's Services menu reach the same engine. The automation workflow covers all three; the full flag reference is in the user guide.

Songs

On macOS 27, Apple's on-device music analysis reads a song's key and tempo, its sections with each one's loudness under the ruler, its bars and beats for the ruler and for snapping, and its four stems: vocal, drums, bass and other. A detected song opens the sidebar's Music group and switches the ruler to bars; the Stems view shows where each stem plays, and stem-gated loudness measures the vocal against the bed. A song's sections can become chapters for a per-section loudness table. The analysis is labelled as Apple's throughout, and the loudness figures stay Specula's own BS.1770-4 measurement.

Standards

Specula implements the actual specifications, not approximations of them.

EBU R 128
loudness compliant
ITU-R BS.1770-4K-weighting filter (two cascaded biquad IIR sections), 100 ms gating blocks, dual gating (−70 LUFS absolute, −10 LU relative), 4× oversampled inter-sample peak detection.
EBU R128Momentary (400 ms window), Short-term (3 s window), Integrated (gated whole-programme), LRA (10-95 percentile range), True Peak (dBTP). Loudness validated against the EBU R128 loudness test set (Tech 3341/3342).
Silero VADNeural voice activity detection (MIT). Runs at 16 kHz on every loaded file, classifies speech/non-speech per 100 ms block, and feeds the speech-gated loudness path.
FluidAudioApache 2.0 Swift wrapper for Silero on Apple platforms. The model ships inside Specula, so speech-gated analysis runs fully offline with no download.

Requirements

macOS
Sonoma 14.0 or later. Music analysis needs macOS 27; the HDR accents need macOS 26 and a display with HDR headroom.
Hardware
Any Mac that runs macOS 14. Apple Silicon recommended for large files; uses Apple's vDSP throughout.

FAQ

Is Specula 2.0 a free update?

Yes, for every Specula license. Specula updates itself through Check for Updates… in the app menu, and 2.0 keeps the settings 1.x had.

Where did the Mode, Dialogue mode and Chapter mode go?

Deliver to replaces the Mode, with five categories in place of four. Dialogue mode is the Speech task (⌘5) and Chapter mode the Chapters task (⌘4). The user guide's Coming from 1.4 section lists where everything went, shortcuts included.

Do I have to analyse a selection to get the verdict?

No. The delivery card judges the whole file as soon as it has loaded, and the reports read the same pass. ⌘Return measures a selection into a card of its own when a part of the file is what you need.

Can Specula check a whole folder?

Yes. File › Check a Folder… checks every audio file in a folder against Deliver to and writes a report per file and a CSV of the rows. The specula command-line tool and the Shortcuts actions run the same engine from a script. See the automation workflow.

Does my audio leave the Mac?

No. Loudness, speech detection and the music analysis run on the Mac. The app's only outbound connections are update checks and license validation, and neither carries audio. See the privacy policy.
