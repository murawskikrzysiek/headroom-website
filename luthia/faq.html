Getting started

What do I need to run Luthia?

A Mac on macOS 26, an iPad on iPadOS 26 or an iPhone on iOS 26, and something the app can hear the guitar through. On the Mac that is any Core Audio input: an audio interface, the built-in microphone, an aggregate device. On iPhone and iPad it is the built-in microphone, a wired headset, a USB or Lightning interface, or a Bluetooth microphone.

Do I need an audio interface, or does the built-in microphone work?

The microphone works for every mode. An interface gives cleaner timing numbers, because the onset detector looks for the pick's attack and a microphone in a room also hears the click, the chair and the fretting hand. With the microphone, headphones for the click keep it out of the measurement; the detector also discounts a click it hears through the mic, but a direct signal is simpler.

Can I use it without a guitar in hand?

Yes. Every fretboard drill has a name task answered from choice chips, and every find task can be answered by tapping the neck on screen. The chord library, the progressions, the editor, the generator, the import preview, the practice planner and the metronome do not need an input at all. What needs the instrument is what listens: Train, the chord drill, the live chord meter and the tuner.

Which guitars and tunings?

Six-, seven- and eight-string guitars and four-, five- and six-string basses. The tuning presets (standard, E♭, D, drop D / C / B, DADGAD, open G / D / E, and the extended-range and bass equivalents) sit in the tuner and drive the Fretboard map, so a 7-string or a bass sees its own neck. The exercise library and the generators are written for a six-string in standard tuning; a bass can still train any of them on its own strings.

Where do I start?

1Pick the input under Settings › Input and press ⌘L (Listen). The meter moves when you play.
2In Train (⌘2) pick one of the built-ins. Open E Pulse is the simplest: one note on the beat.
3⌘K starts the click, Space starts the session. Play in a loop; every note gets its number.
4Then import something of your own: Compose (⌘1) › Import, drop a Guitar Pro or MusicXML file, Train now.

Train & timing

What does the number under each note mean?

How many milliseconds the note landed from where the click said it should: negative is early, positive is late. Two numbers per note: the last pass, and the running average over the session. There is no grade and no score; the numbers are the feedback.

What are spread and lead?

Spread is the standard deviation of the signed deviations: how scattered the notes are around wherever you tend to sit. Lead is the mean: how far ahead of, or behind, the click you sit on average. They are separated on purpose. Most people play a steady few tens of milliseconds ahead of a click, and that lead is not what practice changes; the scatter is. Each loop's row shows the spread as a bar on a fixed 40 ms scale (green to 12 ms, amber to 25, red beyond) and the lead as a dot, and the session panel shows the same two numbers over the whole take.

Every note reads early by roughly the same amount. Is that latency?

Probably not. The click and the input share one clock, the output latency is added to where each beat lands and the input latency is subtracted from each onset, so a constant offset from the device is already taken out. A constant lead of a few tens of milliseconds is the human default when playing to a click: it is reported as the lead and left alone. If the offset is large and the same on every note and every device, the input you selected may not be the one the guitar is on.

Why is a note marked wrong when I played it?

The pitch is read 30, 60 and 100 ms after the attack and the note is what it settled to; the verdict needs the settled read to be confident and the read before it to agree, and it compares pitch classes, not octaves. A verdict still lands on a note that was fretted late (the pick came before the finger) or a note that the previous string was still ringing over. A microphone in a room makes both more likely than a direct signal.

My first note of each loop was not counted.

It is: an onset that the current loop has no note for, played a little early or late around the loop boundary, is offered to the next loop and scored as its first note. A loop is only closed a beat or so after its last note, so a late last note still counts for its own loop. If a first note goes missing every time, it is usually a pick that was quieter than the note before it, which the level rule treats as a re-pick; the count-in is free, so an accented first note costs nothing.

Does the click bleeding into the microphone break the timing?

Not on its own. What an attack may put into the detector's rise envelope is capped relative to the recent hops, so a click through a microphone sets no lasting bar, and each candidate onset is confirmed over a short window before it counts. A pick in the room still wins. With the built-in microphone and the click on speakers, headphones are still the cleaner setup.

How are chords timed?

A chord is one onset. The strum's first string is the attack the detector sees, so a chord exercise is timed the way a single-note one is. The chord's pitches are not checked note by note in Train; the Chords mode's meter and drill do that.

Does the speed trainer mess up the numbers?

No. A ramp steps the tempo exactly on a bar line, or on a loop boundary when it counts loops, and the session's expected-note grid is refreshed from the click's own schedule on every onset, so the numbers stay correct through the change.

Import & export

Which files can I import?

Guitar Pro 7 and 8 (.gp), Guitar Pro 3, 4 and 5 (.gp3, .gp4, .gp5), MusicXML (.musicxml, .mxl), MuseScore 3 and 4 (.mscz, .mscx), MIDI (formats 0 and 1), ASCII tab pasted as text, and Luthia files. Import is free in every format.

Guitar Pro 6 (.gpx) fails. PDF?

Guitar Pro 6's .gpx is not read; open it in Guitar Pro 7 or 8 and save as .gp, which imports in full. MuseScore 1.x files are the same story: save from MuseScore 3 or 4. PDF is not supported; a PDF of tab is a picture of tab, and a scan needs optical music recognition, which Luthia does not do.

What is the "What changed" list?

Everything the import had to simplify to fit the exercise model: a technique that is not carried (harmonics, tapping, tremolo picking, trills, grace notes), a tied chord that could not merge, a second voice, repeat endings that were read but are not shown. Nothing is dropped silently, and the list appears before you save, next to a preview you can play and train.

The import changed my fingering.

Fingering written in the source is kept as written; it also anchors the search that fingers the notes around it. Only notes with no string and fret in the file get one, from a pass over the whole line that keeps the hand in position and favours open strings. In a MuseScore or MusicXML file the fingering usually lives on the linked tab staff, which the importer folds into the notation part, so export the score with its tab staff rather than notation alone.

Can I get an exercise back out?

Yes, any of them, built-ins included. ⌘E, the Export chip or a library row's context menu writes MusicXML (a notation staff and a TAB staff, every technique) for MuseScore, Guitar Pro, Dorico, Sibelius or Soundslice, or a Luthia file that opens verbatim on another device or for another person. Export is never gated.

Do course files open straight from Files, Mail or AirDrop?

Yes. Luthia is registered for every format above, so Open With, a share sheet or a drop on the window lands in the import sheet with the file parsed. The folder field puts a course's files together in the library (a Pro feature; on the free tier the file still imports and trains).

Free & Pro

What is free?

Every mode and every mechanic: the microphone, the notation, the millisecond timing, the metronome and tuner, every import format, export and iCloud sync. The 32 built-in exercises. One exercise of your own at a time, composed, generated or imported; the major and minor pentatonic scales in boxes, major arpeggios and the rhythm pyramid in the generator; the open chord shapes in every quality, the open-majors drill and three progressions; the Notes and Scale overlays on the map and the Notes drill to fret 5; the default practice week with its timer, log and streak.

What does Luthia Pro add?

An unlimited library in folders; all twelve scales, three-notes-per-string fingering and the group and interval patterns; every arpeggio quality, rhythm figures and random grids; every movable chord shape, every drill pool, every progression and your own; chord tones, intervals, octaves and boxes on the map; all six fretboard drills across the whole neck, paced by the click; and the practice planner's Rebuild week, block editing and suggestions. One purchase, $19.99, no subscription.

Does one purchase cover Mac, iPad and iPhone?

Yes. Luthia is one App Store app for all three, so the purchase is one purchase, and Family Sharing is on. Settings › Luthia Pro › Restore Purchase picks it up on a device where it has not appeared yet.

Is there a trial?

The free tier is the trial, with no time limit. Everything that listens, measures and edits runs free, so the question of whether the timing works with your setup is answered before any purchase. Locked items stay visible in the pickers with a lock, so it is clear what Pro would add.

What happens to my exercises if the purchase goes away?

Nothing. Limits cap adding, never reading: whatever the library already holds stays usable, trainable and editable after a refund, or on a device that has not restored the purchase yet while a Pro library syncs onto it. The free tier's one-exercise slot only applies to saving a new one.

Why the App Store, when the other Headroom apps are sold directly?

Because Luthia runs on the iPhone and the iPad as well as the Mac, and a universal purchase across the three only exists on the App Store. Lyra, Audita and Specula are Mac-only and stay direct.

iCloud & data

What syncs?

Your exercises, custom progressions, the practice plan and log, fretboard progress and Train history, through iCloud Drive on your own Apple Account. Sound, the input device, the tuner preset, the drill setup, map settings and notation settings stay per device. The raw audio Train keeps of recent sessions stays on the device it was recorded on.

Two devices edited the same exercise. Which one wins?

The newer edit. Each exercise is its own file, so two devices only collide when they change the same one between syncs. The practice log, fretboard progress and Train history are kept per device and merged on read, so those never collide: an offline session on the iPad counts once it reconnects.

Can I turn sync off?

Settings › iCloud › Sync with iCloud. Off leaves a full local copy on the device; on again merges local edits back in. Signed out of iCloud, the library is local until you sign in.

I do not see a Luthia folder in iCloud Drive.

Correct. The library lives in the app's own iCloud container, which iCloud Drive does not show. Export writes a file anywhere you like if you want one in a folder.

Does anything reach Headroom Studio?

No. Luthia has no accounts and no telemetry, and there is no studio server for it to reach. The only network paths are Apple's: iCloud for sync and the App Store for the purchase. Privacy policy.

Troubleshooting

Listen is on but the meter stays at zero.

Check that microphone permission was granted (System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone on the Mac; Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone on iPhone and iPad). Without it the system delivers silence rather than an error. Then check that Settings › Input points at the device the guitar is plugged into; the refresh button re-reads the list after a hot-plug.

The tuner reads a note when nothing is playing.

The tuner gates on level and confidence, but a hum or a fan on a sensitive microphone can clear the gate. Pick a direct input, or move away from the noise. A reading disappears as soon as a real note is played.

Return does not tap the tempo on my iPad keyboard.

Known gap in the 1.0 builds: Return as tap tempo is wired on the Mac only. Tap in the header, or ⌘⇧K, taps the tempo on the iPad; the editor keys and Space for play and stop do work on the hardware keyboard.

The iPad shows the iPhone layout.

In Split View or Slide Over the app runs its compact shell, the same one as the iPhone. Full screen brings the Mac layout back.

Something else?

hello@headroomstudio.dev. A description of the input and, for a timing question, the exercise and the tempo are the two things that speed up an answer.
